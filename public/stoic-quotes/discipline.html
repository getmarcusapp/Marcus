Stoic Quotes on Discipline and Self-Mastery

Stoic discipline is not endurance for its own sake. It is the claim that character is the residue of what you repeatedly do, so the small settled decisions matter more than the large declared ones. Marcus is the most useful writer here precisely because he is arguing with himself, at dawn, about getting out of bed.

Several of these are instructions rather than observations, which is how they were meant. The Enchiridion is a handbook, and Marcus wrote his notebooks to be reread.

At dawn, when you have trouble getting out of bed, tell yourself: I have to go to work — as a human being. What do I have to complain of, if I'm going to do what I was born for?

Marcus AureliusMeditations V.1

Remember how long thou hast been putting off these things, and how often thou hast received an opportunity from the gods, and yet dost not use it.

Marcus AureliusMeditations II.4trans. George Long

If it is not right, do not do it; if it is not true, do not say it.

Marcus AureliusMeditations XII.17

Such as are thy habitual thoughts, such also will be the character of thy mind; for the soul is dyed by the thoughts.

Marcus AureliusMeditations V.16trans. George Long
