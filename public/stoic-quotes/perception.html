Stoic Quotes on Perception and Judgment

This is the load-bearing idea in Stoic practice, and the one most often flattened in the retelling. The claim is not that nothing bad happens, nor that you should feel less. It is narrower: between an event and your distress sits a judgment you made, usually without noticing, and that judgment is the part available to you.

Note how specific the passages are about where the work happens. It is at the moment of assent, before the reaction, not after it. A paraphrase that relocates it to "how you react" is describing something else: see did Epictetus say it is not what happens to you?

It is not events that disturb people, it is their judgements concerning them.

EpictetusEnchiridion 5trans. Robert Dobbin

If you are pained by anything without, it is not the thing agitates you, but your own judgment concerning the thing; and this it is in your own power to efface.

Marcus AureliusMeditations VIII.47trans. Gerald Rendall

Get rid of the assumption, and therewith you get rid of the sense 'I am an injured man'; get rid of the sense of injury, you get rid of the injury itself.

Marcus AureliusMeditations IV.7trans. Gerald Rendall

Reject the view — and straightway, you are whole. Who hinders the rejection?

Marcus AureliusMeditations XII.25trans. Gerald Rendall

It is not a man who reviles you or strikes you who insults you, but it is your opinion about these things as being insulting.

EpictetusEnchiridion 20

Is the child or wife of another dead? There is no one who would not say, “This is a human accident.” But if any one’s own child happens to die, it is presently, “Alas! how wretched am I?” But it should be remembered how we are affected in hearing the same thing concerning others.

Everything has two handles: one by which it may be borne, another by which it cannot.

EpictetusEnchiridion 43trans. T. W. Higginson

Pain is slight if opinion has added nothing to it; but if, on the other hand, you begin to encourage yourself and say, “It is nothing,—a trifling matter at most; keep a stout heart and it will soon cease”; then in thinking it slight, you will make it slight.

Pain that is past bearing, brings an end; pain that lasts, can be borne. The understanding in abstraction maintains its calm, and the Inner Self is unimpaired.
