On the fountain within

Look within. Within is the fountain of good, and it will ever bubble up, if thou wilt ever dig. — Marcus Aurelius, Meditations VII.59

Marcus wrote the Meditations in Greek, privately, mostly while on campaign on the northern frontier. Nobody was meant to read them. That is worth holding on to when a line like this one sounds like advice: it is a man giving himself an instruction he evidently needed repeating.

The second half is the part that gets dropped. If thou wilt ever dig. The source is not sitting on the surface waiting to be noticed, and the sentence is not a reassurance that you are fine as you are. It is a claim that the work is available and that it is work: nothing arrives without the digging, and the digging does not finish.

What would you find today if you dug rather than waited?
