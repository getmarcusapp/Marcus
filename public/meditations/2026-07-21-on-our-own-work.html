On our own work

"In the morning when thou risest unwillingly, let this thought be present—I am rising to the work of a human being." — Marcus Aurelius, Meditations, Book 5

Marcus Aurelius wrote these lines to himself, not for readers. He was emperor of Rome, dragged from bed each dawn into a life of war on the Danube frontier, plague in the cities, and the endless administration of an empire he had not asked to rule. Book 5 opens with the argument he made against his own reluctance: that a person is made for a particular kind of labor, and that shirking it is a kind of self-betrayal. The work of a human being was, for him, the whole point of being awake.

This week a large survey reported that many audiobook listeners rated an AI multi-voice narration as favorably as, or above, a human reader. After hearing a sample, 65% said they were likely to listen to a full multi-voice AI audiobook, up from 31% beforehand. The finding raises an old question in new clothing. If a machine can voice the words, what exactly was the human doing? Marcus would not have asked whether the reading pleased the audience. He would have asked whether the reading was ours to do.

Which of the tasks you are tempted to hand off is not a burden at all, but the actual work of being a person?
