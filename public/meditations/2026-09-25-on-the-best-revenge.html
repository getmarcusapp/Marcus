On the best revenge

The best revenge is to be unlike him who performed the injury. — Marcus Aurelius, Meditations VI.6

Eleven words, and one of the few lines in the Meditations that reads as though it were written for someone else. Marcus dealt daily with courtiers, informants and a senate that had reason to fear him, and the temptation he is addressing is not abstract.

What makes it more than a consolation is the word revenge. He does not tell himself that the injury does not matter, or that the person who did it should be forgiven, or that he is above minding. He concedes the wish to settle the score and then redirects it, because the ordinary way of settling it requires becoming, briefly, the sort of person who does that thing. The proposal is that you win by declining to be changed by them.

Who has recently made you more like them than you want to be?
