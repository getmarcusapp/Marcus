On daring the difficult

"It is not because things are difficult that we do not dare, it is because we do not dare that things are difficult." — Seneca, Letters to Lucilius, Letter 104

Letter 104 is written on the road. Seneca has left Rome for his country estate to shake off a fever, and the flight becomes the letter's real subject: he admits that changing your surroundings does nothing if you carry the same frightened mind with you. The line about daring comes at the end of a long argument that the obstacles we dread are mostly built by our own hesitation. Seneca is not telling Lucilius the task is easy. He is saying the difficulty and the flinching are the same thing seen twice, and that the flinching comes first.

This week Céline Dion returned to a Paris stage for her first full concert in six years, after a diagnosis that took away the one instrument she could not replace. Dion opened her Paris residency on September 12, her first concert since revealing she had been diagnosed with Stiff Person Syndrome back in 2022. The years away were not idle. They were spent deciding whether to try. What looks from outside like a hard thing accomplished is really a long argument with hesitation, finally won.

What have you called impossible when the honest word was unattempted?
