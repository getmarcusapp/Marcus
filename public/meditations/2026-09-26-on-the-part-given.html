On the part given

For this is your business, to act well the given part; but to choose it, belongs to another. — Epictetus, Enchiridion 17

The line is the close of a single image Epictetus hands his students: you are an actor in a play whose length and role were decided before you walked on. He is answering a complaint he heard often in his classroom, the wish to be cast differently: richer, freer, more admired, spared the humbler part. His reply divides the one thing that is yours from everything that is not. You do not write the play or assign the roles. You govern only the quality of your performance in the part you were handed. Applause, casting, the reviewer's verdict: all of it belongs to another.

This week the Kentucky songwriter Ian Noe released Canyon Falls Trailer Band, a concept album built around an invented group that plays one showcase seeking industry approval and then vanishes, their fate unknown, the performance itself the only thing that remains. It is a strange and honest premise. Most work is like that band. You give the performance. Whether anyone approves, whether it is remembered, whether it opens the door you hoped for: none of that is in your hands. Only how well you play is.

If no one ever approved of the work you did today, would you still have wanted to do it well?
