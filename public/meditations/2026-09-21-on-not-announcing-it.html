On not announcing it

On no occasion call yourself a philosopher, and do not, for the most part, talk among the laity about philosophical principles, but act in accordance with them. — Epictetus, Enchiridion 46

Epictetus follows this with an image about sheep. They do not bring the shepherd grass to prove how much they have eaten; they produce wool and milk, and the eating is evident from that. He was talking to young men who had come to study philosophy and were liable to go home and explain it to everyone.

The warning is about a substitution that is easy to make without noticing. Describing a practice feels like doing it, and it is considerably more comfortable, because an account of yourself can be corrected on the spot while conduct cannot. He is not recommending secrecy. He is pointing out that the only evidence anyone should need is the wool.

What are you currently explaining that you could simply be doing?
