On the measure of a life

"A man's worth is no greater than the worth of his ambitions." — Marcus Aurelius, Meditations

The line arrives in a book Marcus filled with corrections aimed at himself, and it corrects a specific temptation an emperor feels daily: to mistake the scale of one's position for the scale of one's soul. He commanded legions and could reward or ruin men with a sentence. So he keeps writing down the opposite standard. A person is not weighed by titles held or crowds pleased, but by the quality of what he reaches for. Aim at trifles, and you are a trifle, whatever the world calls you.

This week the Venice Film Festival honored George Clooney with its Golden Lion for Lifetime Achievement, a prize that gathers a whole career into a single evening of applause. A room stands; a laudatio is read; the work of decades is summarized in praise. Marcus would not sneer at the honor. He would ask a quieter question beneath it. The tribute measures what a career accomplished. His standard measures what it was for. The two are not the same, and the gap between them is where a person actually lives.

If your worth were set today by the worth of what you are aiming at, and not by anything you have already achieved, where would you stand?
