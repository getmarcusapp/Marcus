On doing it again

"Do not act as if thou wert going to live ten thousand years. Death hangs over thee. While thou livest, while it is in thy power, be good." — Marcus Aurelius, Meditations 4.17

This passage comes at the point in Book Four where Marcus turns from cosmology to a warning against a very particular kind of laziness: the assumption that the good thing can always be done later. He is not urging haste for its own sake. He is arguing against the habit of treating the self we were as sufficient, of coasting on what we once managed. The word he keeps returning to is "now." Not the memory of having been good, but the act itself, while it is still in your power.

Last night, Madonna made her grand return to the MTV Video Music Awards, performing at the show for the first time in 23 years and joined onstage by Sabrina Carpenter and Charli XCX. The easy thing for anyone with that history is to let the old footage do the work: the 2003 stage, the version of yourself people already agreed was great. Marcus is unimpressed by that arrangement. A reputation is a record of past action. It proves nothing about whether you will act well today, when acting is harder and the memory would suffice.

What in your life are you letting a past version of yourself vouch for, instead of doing again, now?
