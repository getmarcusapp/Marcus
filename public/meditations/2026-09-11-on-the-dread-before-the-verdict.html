On the dread before the verdict

"We suffer more often in imagination than in reality." — Seneca, Letters to Lucilius, Letter 13

The line answers a specific complaint. In Letter 13, addressed to Lucilius under the heading later given as "On Groundless Fear," Seneca is arguing with a friend who is rehearsing disasters that have not arrived. His counsel is precise: much of what torments us is borrowed from the future, dread of a thing rather than the thing itself. He tells Lucilius to ask of each fear whether it is certain, and to stop being unhappy in advance of any real cause. The point is not courage in the abstract. It is the specific waste of suffering an event twice, once in the mind and once in fact.

This week the world's filmmakers gathered in Toronto, where the 51st Toronto International Film Festival returns from September 10 to 20, 2026, and the festival's venues serve as fall's most critical awards-season launchpad. A world premiere is a single unrepeatable exposure: months of work meet a room of strangers at once. Most of the fear a maker carries into that theatre concerns a judgment that has not yet been passed. Seneca's question still applies. Is the thing you dread real yet, or only imagined?

What are you suffering today that has not actually happened, and may never?
