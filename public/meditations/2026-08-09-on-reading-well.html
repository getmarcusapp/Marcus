On reading well

"You must linger among a limited number of master thinkers, and digest their works, if you would derive ideas which shall win firm hold in your mind." — Seneca, Letters to Lucilius, Letter 2

Seneca is answering a specific admission from his correspondent Lucilius, who had confessed to wandering restlessly from book to book. Seneca does not praise the appetite. He treats it as a symptom of the same unsettledness that keeps a traveler moving from house to house, never at home anywhere. His prescription is narrow on purpose: return to a few writers, read them slowly, and carry away something each day. The point was not to accumulate books but to be changed by one.

Today is National Book Lovers Day, which in 2026 falls on Sunday, August 9, an occasion to set aside screens and lose oneself in a book. It arrives in a culture that measures reading by the tally: books finished, lists cleared, shelves photographed. Seneca would recognize the restlessness. The question he raises is quieter than "how much have you read." It is whether anything you read last year is still working on you now, still shaping a decision you make today.

Which book have you actually let change you, rather than merely finished?
