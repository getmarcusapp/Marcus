On the unseen

"Everything we hear is an opinion, not a fact. Everything we see is a perspective, not the truth." — Marcus Aurelius, Meditations

Marcus wrote the Meditations as private notes to himself, largely on campaign at the empire's frozen northern frontier. He was a man surrounded by advisers, reports, and appearances, all of them insisting they showed him the world as it was. His discipline was to distrust the surface: to remember that what reached his eyes and ears had already been shaped, filtered, and framed before he received it. Seeing clearly meant first admitting how little seeing alone reveals.

Yesterday, astronomers using the James Webb Space Telescope announced a giant planet in the Beta Pictoris system, one of the most studied stretches of sky we have. The newly identified Beta Pictoris d was discovered not by identifying a bright point of light but by detecting the unique chemical signature of its atmosphere. A world that had escaped every direct look was found by inference, by reading what could not be seen directly. Most of what governs a life works the same way: motives, causes, the weight behind another person's silence. The visible is rarely the whole account.

What in your life have you judged only by its surface, when the truer signal was in something you would have to infer rather than see?
