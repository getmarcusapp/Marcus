On the given term

"Let us postpone nothing. Let us balance life's account every day." — Seneca, Letters to Lucilius, Letter 101

Seneca wrote this letter in response to a single piece of news: the sudden death of Cornelius Senecio, a rising Roman businessman he admired, a man who had gone to bed healthy and prosperous and was gone within a day. The shock of it is what drives the letter. Seneca's argument is not that death is near but that its date is hidden from everyone, which makes the hidden date irrelevant. Senecio had plans stretching years ahead. The plans and the man ended on the same morning. So Seneca refuses the comfort of a long horizon and tells Lucilius to close each day as if it were the settled sum of a life.

This week the actor Bruce Campbell spoke publicly about his cancer. He said his current life expectancy is five years, "give or take," and that he has decided to live with it, not die from it. He is focusing on his work and moving forward with plans to promote an independent film he wrote and directed. Most people are never handed a number. Campbell was, and answered by settling his accounts one working day at a time.

If you knew the length of your term the way Campbell knows his, which task would you stop postponing first?
