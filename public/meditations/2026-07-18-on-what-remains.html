On what remains

"Observe constantly that all things take place by change, and accustom thyself to consider that the nature of the Universe loves nothing so much as to change the things which are and to make new things like them." — Marcus Aurelius, Meditations, Book IV

Marcus wrote the Meditations in the 170s, in army camps along the Danube, during a war he did not want and a plague he could not stop. He was an emperor writing only to himself. Again and again he returned to one consolation: everything he saw would pass, dissolve, and become the raw material of something else. The stars included. This was not despair. It was how he kept his footing.

This week astronomers reported something that would not have surprised him. Four nearby white dwarf stars were found hiding in plain sight beside brighter red dwarf companions, revealed by Hubble's ultraviolet observations, including one just 25 light-years away that took nearly three decades to confirm. A white dwarf is what a star leaves behind after it has spent itself: dense, faint, still there. The bright thing burns out and does not vanish. It changes state and keeps quiet company with what outshines it.

What in your life have you mistaken for gone, when it has only changed into a quieter form?
