On the whole of a life

"As it is with a play, so it is with life: what matters is not how long the acting lasts, but how good it is." — Seneca, Letters from a Stoic, Letter 77

Seneca wrote Letter 77 to talk Lucilius out of a common fear: that a life cut short, or a reputation gone wrong, is a life wasted. His counter was that length is not the measure. A play judged good is not made better by dragging on, and a man is not redeemed or ruined by the last act alone. What counts is the quality of what was done, taken whole.

The Herbert Hoover Presidential Library and Museum reopened this week in West Branch, Iowa, after a long renovation. The museum follows the former president and his wife Lou's life before, during, and after Hoover's one-term presidency from 1929 to 1933. That single term, defined by the Depression, fixed his reputation for decades. But the exhibits reach past it, and his family framed the day around the fuller record. Allan Hoover III called the reopening an opportunity to honor the president's legacy of humanitarian service, innovation, leadership and public service. A life is longer than its worst chapter, and slower to judge than the news that made it.

If you were remembered for the whole of your conduct rather than your hardest year, what would change in how you carry today?
