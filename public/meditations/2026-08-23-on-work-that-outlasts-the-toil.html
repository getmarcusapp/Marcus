On work that outlasts the toil

"If one accomplishes some good though with toil, the toil passes, but the good remains." — Musonius Rufus, That One Should Disdain Hardships

Musonius spoke these words to students who wanted the fruits of philosophy without the discomfort of practicing it. His argument was blunt: no one refuses food because chewing is tiresome, no one turns down health because the training that builds it is hard. The exertion is temporary and forgettable. What it produces is not. He was answering a very human bargain people try to strike, in which they hope the good can be had at a discount, without the labor priced into it.

This week the Camden International Film Festival announced its 2026 lineup, and its executive director framed the moment plainly. The conditions for making independent documentary have rarely been harder, and the need for it has rarely been clearer. Funding has contracted, distribution has narrowed, and filmmakers are being asked to justify work that takes years to do properly; what the 2026 program demonstrates is that they are doing it anyway. The films will screen and the strain behind them will fade. What was made stays made.

What are you doing right now whose difficulty you will forget, but whose result you would not trade away?
