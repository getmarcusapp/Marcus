On the two tests

If it is not right, do not do it; if it is not true, do not say it. — Marcus Aurelius, Meditations XII.17

This sits near the end of the Meditations, in the twelfth and last book, among a run of notes that are shorter and blunter than the earlier ones. Whatever else it is, it is a man late in a long life reducing the thing to its smallest possible form.

Two tests, one for action and one for speech, and neither of them asks about outcome. Not: will this work, will it be noticed, will it be understood, will it cost me. Only whether the act is right and whether the statement is true, both of which you can determine before you move and neither of which depends on anyone else. It is a short sentence with almost nothing in it, which is the point: at this length there is nowhere for a qualification to hide.

What are you about to do or say that would not survive either test?
