A disclosure first, because a comparison is only worth reading if you know who wrote it: we make Marcus, one of the five apps below.

So do not take our word for the others. Every fact about them here comes from their own App Store listings or websites, checked on October 27, 2026, and each section links to its source so you can check it yourself. Where something could not be confirmed, we say so. Prices change, and the App Store sometimes shows different prices to different people, so treat the numbers as a guide.

Most "best Stoic apps" lists you will find are written the same way, by one of the apps on the list. The honest ones say so. Ours does.

## The short answer

These apps are not five versions of one thing. They are different kinds of tool that share a word.

- **You want a full journaling app with optional AI coaching:** stoic.
- **You want guided audio meditation and lessons on the Stoics:** Stoa.
- **You want one good quote a day and very little else:** Stoic Mind.
- **You want something free, with no subscription at all:** Stoic Thoughts.
- **You want a structured daily Stoic practice, morning to evening:** Marcus.

## stoic. journal & mental health

The biggest app here by a distance: a 4.8 rating from more than 36,000 reviews, on the App Store since 2017, and an Apple Editors' Choice. Despite the name, it positions itself as a mental health journal rather than a Stoicism app, and the Stoic material is one flavor among many.

- **What it does:** morning preparation and evening reflection, guided journals and prompts, mood and habit tracking, streaks, meditation and breathing, voice and photo entries, widgets, and sync across iPhone, iPad, Mac and Watch.
- **Cost:** free to journal. The listing shows Premium prices from $6.99 to $39.99, a separate AI tier from $12.99 to $99.99, and a $299 lifetime option. The listing does not say which price is monthly and which is yearly.
- **AI and privacy:** journals are stored on your device, with iCloud backup on Premium. The AI features are powered by OpenAI, and the company says that if you turn them on, your journal entries are sent to its AI providers.
- **Best for:** people who want a polished, general-purpose journal with a Stoic accent and are comfortable with AI reading their entries.

[App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/stoic-journal-mental-health/id1312926037)

## Stoa

The serious one for listening. Stoa is built around guided Stoic meditation: over 100 hours of audio, a three-week introductory course, lessons on Marcus Aurelius, Seneca and Epictetus, and conversations with scholars. It has a 4.8 rating from about 1,000 reviews.

- **What it does:** daily guided meditations, structured lessons, a library of quotes and original texts, a daily quote, and widgets. It does not describe a journaling feature.
- **Cost:** a 7-day free trial of everything. The listing shows weekly, monthly and yearly prices, with yearly options between $39.99 and $79.99. More than an hour of meditation, and the quotes and books, are free. The developers also say that anyone who cannot afford it can email them for a free account, which is worth knowing and rarer than it should be.
- **Privacy:** the App Store privacy label lists data used to track you, including contact info and identifiers.
- **Best for:** people who learn by listening and want the philosophy taught, not just quoted.

[App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/daily-stoic-philosophy-stoa/id1446345567)

## Stoic Mind

The minimalist. One quote a day, a plain-English explanation of it, and a life calendar of your weeks as a memento mori. Its own site says the point is to use it less. It has a 4.8 rating from about 700 reviews.

- **What it does:** a daily quote from the major Stoics, explanations, favorites, the life-in-weeks calendar, reminders, and widgets. No journal.
- **Cost:** the cheapest paid option here. Pro is $4.99 a month, $14.99 a year, or $19.99 for life. The free version includes the daily quote and the calendar, with ads.
- **Privacy:** the privacy label lists usage data used to track you, and third-party advertising.
- **Best for:** beginners who want a daily nudge, not a practice.

[App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/stoic-mind-daily-quotes/id1638576723)

## Stoic Thoughts

A free quote app with nothing to buy. Quotes arrive as notifications. It has a 4.8 rating from about 600 reviews.

- **Cost:** free, with no in-app purchases.
- **Privacy:** the App Store label says no data is collected, though the developer's own privacy policy mentions Firebase analytics and crash reporting. The policy also says the app uses no AI.
- **Worth knowing:** the listing says little about features beyond quotes, and the last update was March 2026.
- **Best for:** anyone who wants Stoic quotes and will not pay for them.

[App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/stoic-thoughts/id1529596834)

## Marcus

Ours. Marcus is a daily practice rather than a library or a journal: the routine the Stoics actually describe, laid out across the day. It launched in 2026, so it is small. At the time of writing it had six ratings, all five stars, which is a promising sample and not yet a meaningful one.

- **What it does:** a morning preparation built on your Stoic Compass (what you are working on and who you are trying to become), a daily reading chosen from a cited library, morning and evening journals, an emotion log with Stoic reframes, a weekly review, seven short guided meditations, and an evening examination modeled on the one Seneca describes.
- **Cost:** your first day is free with no trial or card needed. After that, a 7-day free trial, then $7.99 a month or $39.99 a year, or $79.99 once for lifetime access with nothing to renew.
- **AI and privacy:** your journal never leaves your device, and there is no AI reading your entries. AI is used in one place: to choose the day's reading from the library and write a short reflection on it. Your Compass is the only thing you have written that is sent for that, and your journal entries never are. Every passage the app shows is cited to a work and a section, and we audited the library in 2026 and [published what we found](/misattributed-stoic-quotes).
- **Where it falls short:** no Android version, no widgets yet, and no cloud sync. Your practice lives on one phone, and you back it up yourself.
- **Best for:** people who want to practice Stoicism every day, not read about it.

## What about the Daily Stoic app?

Ryan Holiday's Daily Stoic is a book, a daily email and a podcast. As of October 2026 there is no official Daily Stoic app on the App Store. Several apps with "Daily Stoic" in their names are made by other developers.

Some lists also include apps that have not launched yet. If an app has a waitlist instead of an App Store page, nobody comparing it has used it.

## How to choose

Three questions sort it out quickly.

1. **Do you want to read about Stoicism, or practice it?** Quotes and lessons are a good way in. Practice is where the philosophy actually does anything, which is why the Stoics wrote handbooks and not just treatises.
2. **Do you want to write?** If yes, it is stoic. or Marcus. If not, Stoa or Stoic Mind.
3. **Who should be able to read what you write?** An app that sends entries to an AI provider is a reasonable choice, and it is a choice. Know which kind you are using.

Whichever you pick, the test is the same one Marcus Aurelius set himself: not how much you know, but whether today went a little better than yesterday.
