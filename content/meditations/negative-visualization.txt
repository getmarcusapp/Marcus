This practice asks something of you. It asks you to imagine, briefly and honestly, the absence of things you love.
[[5]]
Not because loss is coming. But because we spend most of our lives looking past what we have, reaching for what we don't.
[[6]]
The Stoics called this negative visualization. Marcus practiced it. Epictetus taught it. It is not pessimism. It is the cultivation of attention.
[[14]]
Begin with something simple. Think of a person in your life who matters to you. Someone you see often enough that you have stopped fully seeing them.
[[21]]
Now imagine, gently, that they are gone. Not forever. Just imagine the space they leave. The silence where their voice was. The absence of something so familiar you had stopped noticing it.
[[41]]
Now let them return.
[[14]]
What is different now?
[[28]]
Epictetus taught his students: Never say about anything, I have lost it, but only, I have given it back. Everything you love is on loan.
[[6]]
Not to create anxiety. To create presence.
[[5]]
The cup you drink from in the morning, on loan. The work that gives your days structure, on loan. The body that carries you, on loan. The people who know your name, on loan.
[[14]]
None of this is morbid. It is simply accurate.
[[14]]
Think now of something in your life that you've been taking for granted. Not with guilt. Just with honesty. Something that was once new, once wanted, that has faded into the background of ordinary days.
[[28]]
