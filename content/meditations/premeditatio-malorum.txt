Find your position. Settle in.
[[13]]
This practice is not about relaxation. It is about preparation. The Stoics called it premeditatio malorum, the premeditation of adversity.
[[5]]
Before the day begins, before anything has gone wrong, you imagine, clearly and honestly, what might.
[[6]]
This sounds like pessimism. It is the opposite. Most suffering is not caused by events themselves. It is caused by the gap between what we expected and what happened. The Stoics understood this. They closed the gap before it could open.
[[13]]
Think of today. Not the whole day, just the next few hours. What is in front of you?
[[27]]
Now ask, without alarm: what could go wrong? Not catastrophically, not dramatically. Just honestly.
[[8]]
A meeting that doesn't land the way you hope. A person who responds with indifference, or hostility. A plan that requires changing. Work that takes longer than expected. Something you were counting on that doesn't come through.
[[13]]
Let one of these feel real for a moment. Don't push it away.
[[40]]
Now ask: if this happens, what would you do? Not what would you feel. What would you do?
[[40]]
You already know. You are more capable of meeting difficulty than you remember in the middle of ease.
[[13]]
Seneca wrote to his friend Lucilius: Exile, the torture of disease, wars, shipwreck. We must think on these.
[[5]]
He was not being dramatic. He was being precise. He meant: do not let the first time you encounter hardship also be the first time you think about it.
[[8]]
You cannot prepare for everything. But you can prepare your response. You can practice the posture of a person who is not destroyed by what does not go to plan.
[[13]]
Now go one layer deeper. Of the things that might go wrong today, which of them are actually in your control? And which are not?
[[40]]
The outcomes often aren't yours. The response always is.
[[8]]
This is not resignation. The Stoics were not passive people. Marcus Aurelius governed an empire in a time of plague and war. Epictetus was enslaved. They acted with full force, with full presence. But they did not attach their stability to the results.
[[13]]
Take a breath.

You have looked at the day honestly. You have not flinched from what might be hard. And you are still here, ready.
[[8]]
That is the practice. Not to avoid difficulty. To meet it having already decided who you intend to be when it arrives.
