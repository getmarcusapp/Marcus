This is a practice for the end of the day. You can listen lying down. If you fall asleep before it ends, that is fine. That is what it is for.
[[12]]
Let your body be heavy. Let the bed, or the floor, take your full weight. There is nothing more to hold up today.
[[20]]

Seneca ended each day the same way. Before sleep he went over the day, honestly and without cruelty, and then he let it go. He wrote: how sweet is the sleep which follows this self-examination?
[[15]]
We will look at the day only briefly. Not to judge it. Just to set it down.
[[10]]
What is one thing you did well today? However small. Let it come, and let yourself have it.
[[35]]
What is one thing you would do differently? Look at it once, clearly. Then put it down. Tomorrow you can begin again.
[[35]]
Is there anything still in your hands? A worry. A task. A conversation. Imagine setting it on a table beside you, where it will wait until morning.
[[35]]
It does not need you tonight. It will still be there tomorrow, and so will you.

Now let your attention move slowly through the body. The forehead, softening. The jaw, loosening. The shoulders, sinking down.

The stomach, soft. The legs, heavy. The feet, still.

Seneca wrote to his friend Lucilius about how to end a day. Let us go to our sleep with joy and gladness; let us say: I have lived; the course which Fortune set for me is finished.
