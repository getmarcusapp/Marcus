Wherever you are, stop for a moment. You don't need to go anywhere for this. That is the point of it.
[[8]]
Let your shoulders drop. Let your hands rest. Take one slow breath, and let it leave on its own.
[[22]]
The middle of the day has a way of scattering us. Messages, requests, small frictions, the next thing and the next. By noon, attention is spread across a dozen places, and none of them is here.
[[11]]
People imagine that rest means getting away. A holiday. A quiet house in the country. Somewhere else.
[[6]]
Marcus Aurelius knew that wish well, and he answered it. Nowhere can man find retirement more peaceful and untroubled than in his own soul.
[[13]]
You carry the place you are looking for. It is not far. It is here, underneath the noise.
[[11]]
Turn your attention inward now. Notice the breath, moving without your help. Notice the steady fact of being here, before any thought about the day arrives.
[[33]]
Now notice the thoughts as they come. A task. A worry. Something someone said. Let each one appear, and let it pass, like a sound outside a window. You do not have to follow it.
[[44]]
Marcus called this inner ground a citadel. In his words: the mind free from passions is a citadel; man has no stronger fortress to which he can fly for refuge and remain impregnable.
[[13]]
Impregnable. Not because nothing reaches you. Because what reaches you does not get to decide who you are.
[[11]]
Bring to mind one thing from this morning that is still pulling at you. Look at it from inside the citadel. It is out there. You are in here.
[[39]]
Ask yourself: what does this actually require of me? Not what it makes me feel. What it requires. Often the answer is smaller than the noise around it.
[[33]]
Rest here a little longer. Nothing to fix. Nothing to solve. Just this inner quiet, which was here before the day began and will be here when it ends.
[[50]]
In a moment you will return to the day. The requests will still be there. The frictions too.
[[6]]
But you know the way back here now. It takes one breath. You can come back as often as you need, and no one can take the door from you.
[[13]]
Take one more slow breath. Then open your eyes, and go back to the work.
