Remember that you will die.

This is not a morbid practice. It is a clarifying one. The Stoics did not contemplate death to make themselves sad. They contemplated it to make themselves honest. To see what is precious. To stop wasting the only resource that does not come back.
[[15]]
Take a breath. Notice your body. That it is a body. That it is finite. That the version of you sitting here right now will not always exist.
[[30]]
Picture, for a moment, your last day. You do not know when it is. You will not know when it is. But it is real, and it is somewhere ahead of you, and it is closer today than it was yesterday.
[[45]]
Marcus Aurelius wrote: Whatever you do or say or think, it is in your power, remember, to take leave of life.
[[6]]
He was not threatening himself. He was reminding himself.
[[6]]
The man who knows he will die today does not waste an hour on resentment. Does not put off the conversation. Does not perform for people whose opinions will not matter in a hundred years, or in a single afternoon, when it ends.
