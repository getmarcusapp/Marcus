Find a comfortable position. Let your body settle, and your breath slow.
[[12]]
The Stoics believed that we are not separate creatures who happen to live near one another. We belong to one another.
[[6]]
Seneca put it plainly to his friend Lucilius. We are the parts of one great body. Nature produced us related to one another, since she created us from the same source and to the same end.
[[12]]
A later Stoic, Hierocles, gave this idea an image. Picture yourself at the center of a set of circles, one around the next, each holding more people than the last. The practice is to draw the outer circles inward.
[[10]]
Begin at the center. Yourself. Your body, breathing, here. Wish yourself well, simply, the way you would wish it for a friend.
[[30]]
Now the first circle. The people closest to you. Family. The ones you live with, or love most. See their faces. Wish them well.
[[35]]
The next circle. Friends, colleagues, neighbors. People you see often, without always noticing them. Bring a few to mind. Wish them well too.
[[35]]
Further out. People you do not know. The person who served you coffee. The driver beside you in traffic. The stranger who passed you today. Their lives are as full as yours, and as difficult.
[[35]]
Now someone hard. A person you are in conflict with, or who has wronged you. You do not have to approve of them. Only remember that they belong to the same great body.
[[30]]
Marcus Aurelius, thinking of the people who frustrated him, wrote this to himself: we are made for co-operation, like the feet, the hands, the eyelids, the upper and the lower rows of teeth. To thwart one another is contrary to nature.
[[12]]
The hands do not resent the feet. They work together because they are one body.
[[10]]
Now the widest circle. Everyone. Every person alive, in every city and every country, waking and sleeping, struggling and hoping. Hold them all, for a moment, as yours.
[[40]]
Come back now to the center. To yourself, breathing. Notice whether the center feels different with the circles drawn in.
[[25]]
Today you will meet people from every one of these circles. Try to treat each one as a part of the same body, because they are.
[[12]]
Take one slow breath, and open your eyes.
