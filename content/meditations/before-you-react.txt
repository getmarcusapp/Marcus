This is a practice for the moment after something has gone wrong. Someone has said something. Done something. And part of you is already answering.
[[8]]
If you are listening to this now, that is the first good decision. You have made a little space.
[[12]]
Sit, or stand, wherever you are. Let your breath slow. Notice your hands, your jaw, your shoulders. Anger lives in the body before it reaches the mouth.
[[25]]
Seneca wrote three books about anger, and his best advice is the simplest. The greatest remedy for anger is delay.
[[12]]
Not suppression. Not pretending it isn't there. Delay. Give the first heat time to pass, so that a judgment can be made instead of a reaction.
[[12]]
So let us delay. Breathe in slowly. And out.
[[18]]
Now look at what happened. Only the facts, as a stranger would describe them. What was said. What was done. Leave out what it meant, for now.
[[37]]
Epictetus gave his students a hard truth for moments like this one. Bear in mind that it is not the man who reviles or strikes you that insults you, but it is your judgement that these men are insulting you.
[[15]]
The words happened out there. The insult is something your mind added. That is not an accusation. It is good news. What your mind added, your mind can examine.
[[12]]
So examine it. What story are you telling about this? That they meant it? That it shows what they think of you? That it cannot be allowed?
[[37]]
Is the story certain? Or is it one of several that would fit the facts?
[[31]]
Epictetus went on: if once you gain time and delay, you will more easily become master of yourself.
[[12]]
You have gained some time. Feel the difference. The heat may still be there, but it is no longer the one deciding.
[[25]]
Now ask what the situation actually needs from you. Not what would feel satisfying. What would be just, and useful, and worthy of the person you mean to be.
[[43]]
Perhaps it needs a word. Perhaps silence. Perhaps nothing at all until tomorrow. You can choose now, because you are choosing, not reacting.
[[18]]
Take one more slow breath. When you return to the moment, return to it slowly.
