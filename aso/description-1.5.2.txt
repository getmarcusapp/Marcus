Most self-improvement apps make you feel good about yourself. Marcus asks whether you are.

Marcus is a daily Stoic practice: an intention in the morning, a passage from the Stoics, and an honest review at night. Not a habit tracker. Not a quote feed. The exercises Marcus Aurelius, Seneca and Epictetus actually practiced, set out so you can do them in a few minutes a day.

THE DAILY PRACTICE

Stoic Compass. Four answers that anchor everything: why you practice, what you are working to overcome, who you are becoming, and the roles you actually hold, as parent, partner, colleague, citizen. You read it each morning, and the daily reading is matched against it, so the practice grows more specific to you the longer you keep it.

Daily reading. One passage each morning from Marcus Aurelius, Epictetus or Seneca, chosen for what you are working on, with a short reflection. It never repeats a passage.

Morning journal. Five prompts, including premeditatio malorum: rehearsing what could go wrong so it cannot ambush you.

Evening journal. The nightly review Seneca described. Where did you act well, where did you fall short, what did you leave undone? Then put it down before sleep.

THE REAL WORDS

Every passage is checked word for word against a named translation. Lines we could not trace to their author are gone, including some of the most shared "Stoic" quotes on the internet. Marcus speaks in Gerald Rendall's plain English translation: "you," not "thou."

ON YOUR HOME SCREEN

Widgets put the day's passage on your Home Screen in three sizes, and a shorter one on your Lock Screen, so the practice meets you before anything else does.

WHEN YOU WANT MORE

Guided meditations. Fifteen voiced sessions of eight to ten minutes, with real silence to practice in: the View From Above, Premeditatio Malorum, Before You React, What Frightens Us, Amor Fati, the Inner Citadel, Memento Mori, the Evening Examination and more. Play one full screen, then control it from your Lock Screen.

Emotion logger. When anger, anxiety or shame takes hold, log it, name the thinking error behind it, and read the Stoic reframe. The founders of cognitive behavioral therapy cited Epictetus for a reason.

Mid-day pause. One question, held for a breath, to catch what you have already agreed to before it hardens into a judgment.

Weekly review. Once a week, look at the whole: the virtue you most embodied, the one you fell short on, and the patterns that kept returning.

Saved lines and The Stoics. Keep the passages you need, and meet the twelve figures behind them, in the order they lived.

BUILT DIFFERENTLY

Missing a day does not erase you. Your longest streak and total days stay. The current streak resets, and you begin again. That is the Stoic response to failure: not shame, the return.

No account required. Your journal never leaves your device. Practice time can count toward Apple Health as mindful minutes, if you choose. The only thing collected is anonymous, aggregate usage, never tied to you.
